# Separate algebraic Fock constructions from analytic realizations

Keep occupation-space algebra, basis-independent fermionic algebra, and Hilbert-space completion as distinct representations. Common's algebraic Fock space is the finitely supported vector space `Config →₀ ℂ`; fermionic `AlgebraicFock` is the exterior algebra of the one-particle space; completed occupation space is an ℓ² space.

The exterior algebra supports basis-independent creation and contraction, while occupation coordinates support explicit operator calculations. Relate these through proved maps and equivalences after supplying the appropriate basis. Algebraic identities alone do not establish boundedness, domain closure, or convergence on a completion.

For infinite-mode fermions, use `ℓ²(Finset Mode, ℂ)` with algebraic finite-particle vectors as a dense core. Keep creation and annihilation bounded where the CAR justify it; model genuinely unbounded diagonal Hamiltonians with explicit maximal domains and closed operators, not continuous linear maps. State the topology of finite-mode approximation at each result: strong convergence of coordinate projections, weak convergence of Gibbs states against bounded operators, and no implied trace-norm convergence.

For finite configurations, retain both the continuous-operator realization on `Config → ℂ` used by operator integration and the Hilbert realization on `EuclideanSpace ℂ Config` used by adjoints and density expectations. The extra bridges preserve the structures needed by each consumer instead of treating finite-dimensional representations as definitionally interchangeable.

Evidence: [occupation algebra](../../LeanCondensedMatter/SecondQuantization/Common/Algebra/AlgebraicFock.lean), [exterior algebra](../../LeanCondensedMatter/SecondQuantization/Fermionic/Algebra/AlgebraicFock/Basic.lean), [generic completed Fock core](../../LeanCondensedMatter/SecondQuantization/Common/CompletedSpace/Basic.lean), [generic weighted diagonal operators](../../LeanCondensedMatter/SecondQuantization/Common/CompletedSpace/Diagonal.lean), [fermionic completed specialization](../../LeanCondensedMatter/SecondQuantization/Fermionic/CompletedSpace/Basic.lean), [finite analytic realization](../../LeanCondensedMatter/SecondQuantization/Common/Perturbation/FiniteAnalyticBridge.lean), and [finite Hilbert realization](../../LeanCondensedMatter/SecondQuantization/Common/Algebra/FiniteHilbertOperator.lean).

## Historical evidence

[Issue #1527](https://github.com/naoki-cpp/LeanCondensedMatter/issues/1527) extracted configuration-independent completed-Fock mathematics to `Common.CompletedSpace`: `ℓ²(Config, ℂ)`, the finite-support dense core and basis extensionality, coordinate projections, maximal weighted diagonal operators and their closed/adjoint/self-adjoint theory, and finite compatibility. State-independent finite-Hilbert representation moved below thermal ownership into `Common.Algebra`. Fermionic retains occupation, toggle/sign, CAR, free-energy, ladder, mode-support/convergence, and thermal specializations. This commonization was justified by actual duplicate consumers; no generic mode-support hierarchy or bosonic mirror was introduced.

[Issue #554](https://github.com/naoki-cpp/LeanCondensedMatter/issues/554) removed inherited `[Fintype Mode]` from the foundational mode-label and finite-support occupation/Fock layers. Keep arbitrary mode types at the algebraic root; require finiteness only downstream where a theorem enumerates all modes/configurations, constructs a finite Hilbert realization, or takes a finite thermal/diagram sum. Removing a false core restriction does not provide the summability or boundedness needed by an infinite analytic construction.

[Issue #532](https://github.com/naoki-cpp/LeanCondensedMatter/issues/532) explicitly left occupation-basis Fock space and basis-independent exterior-algebra Fock separate until a chosen-basis comparison theorem exists. Generic basis-state injectivity and linear independence can be shared for arbitrary configuration types without turning the occupation basis into a second creation/annihilation abstraction. A representation merger must be justified by a proved bridge, not visual similarity.

[Issue #524](https://github.com/naoki-cpp/LeanCondensedMatter/issues/524) formalizes the basis-independent fermionic field layer over an arbitrary one-particle Hilbert space using smeared creation/annihilation fields, exterior-algebra finite-particle states, and `dΓ`. Keep point fields out of the foundational API because they are operator-valued distributions; relate the new algebraic representation to occupation coordinates only through a chosen-basis bridge. The completed Fock representation remains a separate analytic layer, and the conductivity connection uses an explicitly bounded specialization.

[Issue #440](https://github.com/naoki-cpp/LeanCondensedMatter/issues/440) completed the fermionic representation boundary with `ℓ²(Finset Mode, ℂ)`, a dense algebraic core, bounded ladder operators, and closed maximal-domain diagonal Hamiltonians. It proves finite-mode equivalence with the existing finite Hilbert-Fock realization and separately states the strong-projection and weak-state approximation results; neither is upgraded to trace-norm convergence. The completed-space roadmap is the implementation guide, while these representation and topology distinctions are the durable choices.

[Issue #435](https://github.com/naoki-cpp/LeanCondensedMatter/issues/435) carries the algebraic/analytic separation through arbitrary finite Dyson order on infinite bosonic occupation types. Reconstruct each basis column over its finite reachable support, integrate its coordinates, and prove the resulting operator coefficient agrees coordinatewise; specialize back to the established finite-configuration implementation through a bridge. This avoids assuming a finite output basis or claiming unbounded operators are bounded.

[Issue #319](https://github.com/naoki-cpp/LeanCondensedMatter/issues/319) treated the relationship between the existing coefficientwise integral and Mathlib's Bochner integral as a theorem to establish before migration. It permitted proving agreement on basis vectors or matrix coefficients if a direct operator equality was too costly, but required packaging a usable bridge. The old algebraic definition was not to be deleted while existing Dyson and diagram proofs still depended on its coefficient formula.

[Issue #373](https://github.com/naoki-cpp/LeanCondensedMatter/issues/373) distinguishes a finite-support vector from a finite configuration type: every `Finsupp` vector has finite support even when `Config` is infinite. Therefore matrix-coefficient identities and algebraic evolution laws should not acquire `[Fintype Config]` merely because one proof expands a composition over all configurations. Full finite sums remain appropriate where a trace or complete finite enumeration is genuinely required.

[Issue #315](https://github.com/naoki-cpp/LeanCondensedMatter/issues/315) rejected assigning a global norm or topology to algebraic Fock space just to prove finite-dimensional convergence. It retained the coefficientwise interval integral until a compatibility theorem with Mathlib's Bochner interval integral was established, so existing algebraic Dyson proofs did not depend on replacing their semantics. This makes the analytic realization an adapter with a proved bridge.

[Issue #307](https://github.com/naoki-cpp/LeanCondensedMatter/issues/307) made the analytic boundary concrete: finite-configuration endomorphisms are transported to `Config → ℂ` for continuity, operator norms, and Bochner integration, while the source remains algebraic `Config →₀ ℂ`. This is distinct from the Euclidean Hilbert realization used for adjoints and density expectations. The coefficientwise algebraic integral is related to the analytic integral by a proved compatibility theorem rather than by asserting that the source already has the needed topology.
