# Separate thermal states from the pairing recursion

For bounded observables, use the canonical density-operator expectation; finite trace ratios and occupation-coordinate sums are representations connected to it by explicit bridges. A normalized functional with arbitrary complex weights has no automatic Gibbs-state interpretation.

Prove the finite-temperature Bloch–de Dominicis expansion through `ExpectationPairingRecursion`, which accepts an ordered expectation, pair values, admissibility, empty-product normalization, and a first-pair recurrence. Representation-specific implementations supply the KMS/exchange and analytic arguments.

Share the finite scalar-exchange peel at `Analysis.ScalarExchange`, where its recurrence needs only an associative unital complex algebra and a scalar exchange law. Bosonic and completed-fermionic BDD pipelines specialize it at `ζ = +1` and `ζ = -1`; keep operator-product conversion, CAR/CCR realization, and indexed-peel decomposition in their representation-specific consumers, private unless another semantic owner emerges.

For the finite free-fermion thermal Green function, derive mixed and anomalous contractions from the retained BDD two-point API, then compute closed forms directly from the canonical density-state expectation. Do not route the thermal Green-function API back through a weighted occupation-coordinate representation once its BDD contraction kernels are available.

Keep finite Gibbs coordinate and trace-ratio facts in `Common.Thermal.FiniteGibbsCoordinate`, upstream of the Bloch–de Dominicis pairing subtree. BDD owns the pairing-specific expectation, KMS, and peeling contracts; perturbation and thermal consumers should not import a BDD implementation module just to obtain Boltzmann weights or finite trace formulas.

Keep the finite complex-coordinate Boltzmann lane separate from the spectral/pure-point Gibbs lane. `FreeBoltzmannCore` serves finite-coordinate perturbation and partition-series consumers; representation-independent summability belongs under `PurePointSummability`; the completed Fock density is the `completedFreeGibbsDensityOperator` specialization of `QuantumTheory.Gibbs.PurePoint`. Completed BDD and mode-truncation results consume that named state directly rather than depending on finite-coordinate Boltzmann infrastructure.

Share the formal finite-mode free grand-product, unit-constant, formal-log, and connected-cycle backend once at `QuantumTheory.Gibbs.FreeExchangeCycleSeries`, parameterized only by the physical exchange cases `ζ = +1` and `ζ = -1`. Bosonic and fermionic thermal modules may keep domain-facing names and final connected-cycle endpoints as specializations; remove intermediate statistics-specific log wrappers when they only expose a duplicated proof pipeline. The fermionic determinant identity remains fermionic and bosonic occupation-space convergence remains bosonic. These identities are formal: do not evaluate at `t = 1` or infer analytic convergence, and do not widen the product to arbitrary complex exchange weights without explicit algebraic hypotheses.

For genuinely infinite-dimensional equilibrium, take a bounded positive heat operator `Kβ` with explicit spectral-trace-class and nonzero hypotheses, then use `DensityOperator.normalizePositive` as the canonical state boundary. The assertion `Kβ = exp (-β H)` for a semibounded unbounded self-adjoint Hamiltonian belongs in the domain-aware `Analysis` track and must retain the Hamiltonian domain and lower-bound assumptions. It requires a justified bounded functional calculus or equivalent heat-semigroup construction; do not define it by a formal power series of unbounded products or infer it by analytically continuing the real-time Stone evolution. Keep the pure-point Gibbs construction as a spectral-data specialization: when `Kβ` acts on a Hilbert basis by the corresponding Boltzmann factors, the basis action and bundled spectral-trace-class data imply Boltzmann summability and identify its normalized density operator with the existing pure-point state; do not add a duplicate summability premise or a second heat-state wrapper. Do not make general spectral data a competing Gibbs interface until a genuine spectral-measure or functional-calculus API can support it.

Do not infer `SpectralTraceClass Kβ` from semiboundedness or compact resolvent alone. A Hamiltonian-to-heat consumer must establish heat-operator compactness and summability of its Boltzmann eigenvalues under explicit spectral-growth or equivalent hypotheses before using the existing Gibbs normalization; stronger general trace-class or nuclearity claims require their own operator-ideal foundations.

Positive spectral-trace-class normalization belongs to `DensityOperator`, independently of Gibbs or spectral-data representations. Gibbs constructors consume that canonical normalization rather than rebuilding positivity, summability, trace-class data, or trace-one proofs. For the bounded-Hamiltonian Gibbs operator, compactness together with a nontrivial Hilbert space suffices: invertibility supplies nonzeroness, and positivity plus spectral-trace-class data supplies strictly positive trace, so callers do not thread a separate partition-function nonzero premise through Gibbs theorems.

Keep `Fermionic.Thermal` APIs tied to actual Gibbs, KMS, or thermal-expectation semantics. Arbitrary complex-weight occupation moments and cumulants that do not use a Gibbs state, inverse temperature, or Hamiltonian belong in generic combinatorics or a private consumer proof, not in the thermal umbrella without an independent thermal contract.

For unbounded observables, keep operator-domain and eigenvalue facts in the completed representation, then add an integrability-aware Gibbs expectation only for a concrete observable with an actual consumer. The completed total-number expectation is such an endpoint; do not retain a generic arbitrary-diagonal thermal expectation API. Generic unbounded energy expectation remains in `QuantumTheory.Gibbs.PurePointExpectation`, and bounded observables continue through the canonical density-state expectation.

For pure-point Gibbs thermodynamics, derive entropy and Helmholtz identities from the generic diagonal Gibbs probabilities under the explicit `PurePointGibbsEnergyIntegrable` condition. Finite free-fermion thermodynamics should specialize the generic identity and prove only its model-specific partition-function/mean-energy factorization and binary-entropy endpoint; do not reconstruct the configuration-probability von Neumann-entropy bridge in `FreeEntropy`. Do not represent potentially unbounded energy as a bounded observable or add a parallel state or unbounded-observable abstraction for these scalar identities.

Do not extend the bounded noncommuting Gibbs variational theorem to unbounded pure-point data by replacing only the normalized state. `PurePointGibbsEnergyIntegrable` controls the Gibbs distribution, while each competing diagonal state must independently provide normalized probabilities and absolute energy integrability. The countable diagonal variational theorem uses that competitor contract and identifies the normalized Gibbs probabilities as its unique equality case; a fully quantum noncommuting extension requires a competitor-side domain/finite-energy condition and suitable unbounded relative-entropy, form-domain, or lower-semicontinuity machinery. Keep bounded Gibbs uniqueness tied to its Peierls–Bogoliubov equality argument until those foundations exist.

Share the scalar Gibbs inequality and countable entropy-comparison summation below the operator-specific equality layer. The pure-point variational and uniqueness proofs may reuse those scalar/Analysis results, but remain independent of bounded Gibbs equality machinery; derive finite entropy from normalization, energy integrability, and Gibbs summability instead of storing a redundant entropy-summability witness.

This avoids rebuilding the pairing induction for each representation and keeps finite-dimensional assumptions out of the generic theorem. Its cost is an explicit admissibility contract: the generic expansion cannot discharge summability, operator-domain, or KMS obligations. Unbounded observables need a separate integrability-aware expectation rather than being passed to the bounded density-state API.

Within a representation-specific thermal stack, group completed results by stable mathematical responsibility (Gibbs representation, Bloch–de Dominicis pairing, mode-truncation convergence) rather than by the chronological order of proof steps. Keep reusable pairing induction in Common and remove proof-stage modules that have no independent semantic contract, while preserving the public theorem statements.

Evidence: [recursion contract and theorem](../../LeanCondensedMatter/SecondQuantization/Common/Thermal/BlochDeDominicis/ExpectationRecursion.lean), [finite Gibbs bridge](../../LeanCondensedMatter/SecondQuantization/Common/Thermal/FiniteGibbsExpectationBridge.lean), [canonical pure-point Gibbs states](../../LeanCondensedMatter/QuantumTheory/Gibbs/PurePoint.lean), [completed representation umbrella](../../LeanCondensedMatter/SecondQuantization/Fermionic/CompletedSpace.lean), [fermionic thermal umbrella](../../LeanCondensedMatter/SecondQuantization/Fermionic/Thermal.lean), and [thermal architecture](../../notes/roadmaps/thermal-expectation-architecture.md).
